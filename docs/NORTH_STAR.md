# ROAD BRAIN — North Star

## One-sentence definition

**Behavioral conditioning disguised as brain rot.**

ROAD BRAIN is a recurring short-form transmission system that drills simple road etiquette and safety behaviour into viewers through deadpan public-information graphics, escalating institutional failure and absurd visual punishment.

## Master line

> **DRIVE PREDICTABLY.**

This is the project’s thesis, not merely an end card.

## The central creative law

**The joke may be complicated. The instruction cannot.**

A viewer should always be able to extract the road rule even if they miss every reference, glitch, character gag and system message.

## What ROAD BRAIN should feel like

- A real instructional graphic that has become sentient.
- Indian driving-school diagram × public-information signage × Japanese instruction manual × corrupted internet transmission.
- Institutional information rendered through internet contamination.
- Mundane driving behaviour treated with absurd psychic significance.
- A bureaucracy with a nervous system.

## What ROAD BRAIN is not

- A generic glitch-art page.
- A polished cyberpunk brand.
- An anime imitation.
- A conventional road-safety PSA.
- A lecture.
- A collection of random meme edits.
- A place where visual style makes the rule harder to understand.

## Locked creative principles

### 1. Normality is infrastructural
The clean state should be flat, diagrammatic and almost boring. The road is geometry. Cars are symbols. Captions are bureaucratic.

### 2. Bad behaviour introduces colour and instability
The normal world stays nearly black/white/asphalt. Amber, red, psychic violet and aggressive effects arrive when the system detects nonsense.

### 3. The violation gets surreal; the correction stays boring
Never make correct driving feel heroic. Correct behaviour should feel ordinary, stable and almost anticlimactic.

### 4. The transmission can fail; the lesson cannot
Glitches may distort the surrounding system, but the final rule card must remain pristine and readable.

### 5. Repetition is conditioning
Recurring audio, caption grammar, timing states and end cards should make the series recognizable before the viewer reads the account name.

### 6. Each episode breaks a different subsystem
Avoid repeating the same visual joke.

Current pattern:
- EP001: **geometry failure**
- EP002: **vision failure**
- EP003: **world-model / map failure**

### 7. Escalation should be structured, not random
Corruption has an arc: clean → pressured → broken → collapsing → calm/correct.

### 8. Tiny recurring errors create lore
A repeated fault is more interesting than random noise. The institution should have recognizable defects.

## Voice

ROAD BRAIN speaks in two voices:

### The authority
Short, literal, high-legibility instruction.

Examples:
- `INDICATE. THEN MOVE.`
- `DIM YOUR HIGH BEAMS.`
- `THE SHOULDER IS NOT A LANE.`
- `DRIVE PREDICTABLY.`

### The machine operating the authority
Dry system metadata, diagnostics and increasingly unhinged classifications.

Examples:
- `UNSIGNALLED LATERAL MOVEMENT DETECTED`
- `VISUAL INPUT DEGRADED`
- `LIGHT SOURCE: LOCAL STAR`
- `ROAD WIDTH: INFINITE`
- `TELEPATHY NOT SUPPORTED`

## Recurring character logic

Characters are personifications of road infrastructure and bad behaviour, not a conventional mascot cast.

Current archetypes:
- Indicator — Dormant / Ignored / Divine
- Right Lane Raja
- Shoulder Entrepreneur
- High-Beam / Local Star entity
- Zebra Crossing Final Boss
- Blind Spot Demon
- Auto Rickshaw Quantum State

Use characters sparingly. The system itself is the main character.

## Success test

A ROAD BRAIN episode succeeds if:
1. the rule is understood immediately;
2. the violation feels mundane before becoming absurd;
3. the escalation has a clear arc;
4. the correction feels cleaner than everything before it;
5. the piece is sendable even to someone who has never seen ROAD BRAIN;
6. the viewer could recognize another ROAD BRAIN transmission from its grammar alone.
